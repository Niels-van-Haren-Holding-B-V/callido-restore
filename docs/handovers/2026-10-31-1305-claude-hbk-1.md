**Found: three facts about the pool that the placer did not know**
1. **Content dedup leaves phantom records.** When a file of two or more chunks is backed up again (another share, a
   move, a later run) its record gets fresh chunk ids but nothing is appended to any bucket (99-100 % of shared tags with
   two or more chunks have fewer positions than copies; one-chunk files only 22 %). The record streams list 240.5 M
   chunks, the bucket indexes hold 189.8 M; the later copies of tagged files are 139,804 records / 46.0 M chunks of the
   difference, and 5,541 tagged records / 17.4 M chunks are copies of *untagged* originals (their content sits in a bucket
   older than their ids). With both kinds removed the streams hold 177 M physical chunks. The old placer assumed every
   listed chunk is stored, so every duplicate shifted the stream offsets after it: that is where the deficits, the owner
   conflicts and the unowned buckets came from.
2. **Bucket ids are reused; the index revision is the clock.** Dir 2 holds buckets with revision 2 next to revision 248,
   and a writer's anchored buckets are in time order by `(rev, bucket id)` in 99.4 % of steps but by bucket id alone in
   98 % (the exceptions are reused ids). Chunk ids are monotone in time (revision 2 ≈ ids 0-33 M, 298 ≈ 43-118 M,
   the newest ≈ 250 M), so "record id beyond what existed at the bucket's revision" identifies a phantom of an untagged copy.
3. **The stream-offset model holds once phantoms are removed.** Per writer, a pin = (bucket, stream offset of the first
   entry) from a unique anchor. In time order: 13,911 writer-bucket pins agree on the offset against 538 that do not,
   11,784 consecutive pinned buckets line up with zero slack, 0 negative gaps. Per revision band the free (anchorless)
   entries match what the pins require from them: 25.5/25.6 M, 7.3/8.3 M, 31.1/32.6 M, 26.4/26.4 M, 12.2/12.2 M; the
   revision-298 band has 25.6 M free against 10.2 M needed (the initial backup's files that no snapshot of ours lists).
   Anchorless buckets are the interiors of large files, where most of the bytes are (1,593 files > 100 MB).

**Prototype (scratchpad, 12 s on the real data)**: phantom aliasing, pins, a time-ordered chain per writer, a greedy
split of the free buckets between consecutive pins by entry count, placement in stream order. Result: 31,386 buckets
owned, 587,897 of 845,240 records placed (72.5 % of tagged records, 335,388 of them ending exactly at the next anchor),
but only 57.6 % of bytes and 696 of 1,593 files > 100 MB: the greedy split has only entry counts to tell writers apart,
and large files in the same time window compete for the same free buckets.
